### What the videos actually teach

These are notes from the spoken auto-captions of Ahrefs' [two-hour compilation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs), which contains the playlist lessons in order. Auto-captions can mistranscribe names and numbers. The examples below are the instructor's examples, followed by the lesson for Mosaic; they are not claims that the example statistics apply to this hostel.

1. **SEO basics ([1:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=85s)).** Sam Oh describes a search engine as an index built by crawlers following links. Pages must be discoverable, indexed and relevant to a query. He presents organic search as a compounding traffic channel and says that backlinks, search intent and content quality work together. A short answer that fully solves a question can beat a long page that does not. For Mosaic: measure qualified hostel searches and bookings, not word count.
2. **Choosing keywords ([7:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=474s)).** His four checks are search demand, total traffic potential for the *topic* (a page can rank for many related queries), business value, and ability to match intent and compete. A high-volume “kilometers to miles” query may produce few site clicks because Google answers it directly. A golf-club seller scores “buy used golf clubs” higher than a golf movie review. For Mosaic: “hostels near Assi Ghat” has both observed demand and plausible booking value; general trivia may not.
3. **Searcher intent ([15:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=939s)).** Examine the current results for the **three Cs**: content type (product page, article, tool), format (comparison, how-to, list), and angle (for beginners, current year, budget). The video contrasts a “best golf shoes” comparison query with shopping and how-to queries. For Mosaic: a “best hostels” guide should genuinely compare choices; “book Mosaic Hostel” should lead directly to rooms and booking terms.
4. **Finding keywords ([19:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=1191s)).** Begin with broad seed topics, expand into related questions and modifiers, inspect competitors' top pages, then run candidates through the four checks. The instructor uses Ahrefs Keyword Explorer but explicitly allows other tools and a free route. For Mosaic: start with existing GSC queries and competing hostel pages before buying software or publishing new posts.
5. **Ranking difficulty ([31:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=1861s)).** Compare intent fit, backlink strength of ranking *pages*, overall site authority, and the topical focus of sites in the results. He treats keyword-difficulty scores as a starting estimate, not a verdict, and advises looking for weak or mismatched results. For Mosaic: a specific Assi Ghat hostel query is a more realistic initial contest than a generic India travel term; the actual SERP still needs inspection.
6. **On-page principles ([40:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=2428s)).** The main job is satisfying intent, then making the page understandable with title, description, headings and natural wording. He explicitly rejects repeating a keyword a fixed number of times; one strong page can rank for many natural variants. For Mosaic: write useful room and location facts once, in language guests use, without stuffing “hostel near Assi Ghat” into every sentence.
7. **Optimizing a page ([46:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=2786s)).** Inspect the *relevant* top results, discard outliers, identify expected subtopics, write a clear title and description, add internal links to and from related pages, use descriptive image alt text, and make the copy easy to read. His golf-set example identifies a comparison format and beginner angle; he also notes that Google may rewrite descriptions, so they deserve care but not endless polishing. For Mosaic: improve the existing hostel guide with specific, verifiable Assi/room information and natural links; avoid creating near-duplicate pages.
8. **Why links matter ([59:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=3579s)).** A backlink is another site's link to a page. The lesson emphasizes that links arise more naturally when a useful resource, real relationship or credible source gives someone a reason to cite it. The instructor distinguishes this from merely asking strangers to link. For Mosaic: local expertise, original photos or a useful guide can earn a reference; a bought directory placement has little guest value.
9. **Three link strategies ([1:04:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=3878s)).** The video contrasts manually creating links, buying links, and earning links. It warns that paid link schemes violate Google's rules and recommends earning attention through outreach, media sourcing and genuinely linkable resources. For Mosaic: pursue editorial local/travel mentions and legitimate listings; do not buy ranking links.
10. **What makes a good backlink ([1:08:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=4106s)).** The five considerations are relevance, authority of the *linking page*, anchor text, `rel` attributes, and placement/prominence. A contextual link in a relevant article has a stronger rationale than a random footer link. Manipulated exact-match anchors can look unnatural; `sponsored` and `ugc` identify certain link relationships. For Mosaic: evaluate a prospective travel or Varanasi reference for relevance and real readership, not just a domain score.
11. **Beginner link tactics ([1:16:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=4583s)).** The workflow is prospecting, vetting, then outreach. The course discusses journalist-source requests, guest contributions and improving an existing resource before approaching pages that link to a weaker one. It expects substantial rejection and calls for a credible reason to pitch. For Mosaic: prepare one useful local asset before contacting suitable travel writers; do not send mass link requests.
12. **Blogger outreach ([1:30:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=5450s)).** Generic blasts fail because the recipient sees no specific benefit. Find the actual author/editor, show that you read their work, establish why you are qualified, make a relevant suggestion, and end with a low-pressure question. Spend this effort on the site's best content, not every ordinary page. For Mosaic: a tailored pitch about a measured Assi route or original neighborhood guide is plausible; this requires a real asset and the owner's authorization to contact anyone.
13. **Technical SEO basics ([1:43:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=6208s)).** The lesson explains crawlability and indexability: robots.txt governs crawling, a sitemap lists important URLs, and canonical tags indicate a preferred duplicate URL. It recommends checking these first when a valuable page is absent from search. For Mosaic: the controls exist, so investigate specific excluded pages in GSC before adding more SEO files.
14. **Technical maintenance ([1:51:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xsVTqzratPs&t=6660s)).** Keep a logical page hierarchy with useful internal links, avoid slow pages with caching and compressed images, and run periodic crawls to catch broken links, orphan pages, duplicates and redirect chains. The instructor says not to obsess over milliseconds or fix a generic list of 50 issues. For Mosaic: maintain the current static structure and use the existing weekly audit to fix issues that affect useful pages.

**Newer AI-search course, from spoken captions:** In [Ahrefs' 2026 AEO course](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uza9GX0E2mw), Sam Oh explains that AI answers can run several related searches and cite different domains by platform. He proposes auditing which prompts mention the brand, creating original and current answers, earning third-party mentions, checking crawler access, and measuring referrals and conversions. His numerical studies and conversion examples are Ahrefs' claims, not hostel forecasts. For Mosaic, the low-risk application is to publish accurate first-hand property and neighborhood facts, keep important pages crawlable, and track actual referrals. Google's [AI features guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) confirms there is no extra AI schema or machine-readable file required for Google AI search.

## Practical checklist distilled from the courses

1. **Choose searches that can lead to a stay.** Map “hostel in Varanasi,” “hostels near Assi Ghat,” “female dorm near Assi,” and brand searches to one best-fitting commercial page each. Use the existing GSC data before a paid keyword tool.
2. **Match each page to the visitor's intent.** A hostel search needs location, rooms, real prices/terms, photos, reviews and a clear booking path; a travel-guide search needs a direct answer, specific route/timing advice and relevant internal links.
3. **Prove local relevance.** Keep the Business Profile verified and accurate; use authentic room and location photos, genuine reviews, and consistent address, phone and booking details across trusted platforms.
4. **Earn prominence.** Seek editorial mentions, useful partnerships and genuine citations that travelers can discover. Buy neither links nor reviews.
5. **Keep crawl/index foundations sound.** Maintain clean canonical URLs, crawlable textual content, helpful links, sitemap and accessible mobile pages. Test important URLs in Search Console after changes.
6. **Give AI answers something specific to cite.** Publish original property facts, measured distances, local knowledge and clear answers. Google's [AI features guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says normal SEO practices apply; special AI schema or text files are not required.
7. **Measure bookings, then iterate.** Improve pages that show impressions but few clicks, or clicks but few bookings; change one substantial element at a time and allow enough time for recrawling and a fair comparison.

## Advice to reject or correct

- **No guaranteed #1 everywhere.** Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) states there is no secret that automatically ranks a site first. Its [local ranking guide](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091) says local results depend partly on the searcher's distance and cannot be bought into a higher position.
- **Do not expect stars from Mosaic's own review markup.** An older ranked-fixes report recommends `aggregateRating` as a branded star-snippet tactic. Google says local-business reviews are eligible for review snippets only on sites reviewing *other* businesses; self-serving markup on the business's own site does not earn this feature. Review facts should still be accurate and visible. Source: [Google review snippet rules](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet).
- **Do not use Google's Indexing API for hostel/blog pages.** The Oct 1 report says ordinary changed URLs were notified through this API. Google restricts it to `JobPosting` and `BroadcastEvent` inside `VideoObject`. Use sitemap submission and URL Inspection for important ordinary pages. Source: [Google Indexing API rules](https://developers.google.com/search/apis/indexing-api/v3/using-api).
- **Do not count FAQ markup, `llms.txt`, repeated `lastmod` changes or an SEO score of 100 as ranking wins.** Keep only truthful, useful data. Google says there are no special files or schema required for its AI features; a lab audit does not measure search demand or authority.
