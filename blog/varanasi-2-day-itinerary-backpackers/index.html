2-Day Varanasi Itinerary for Backpackers

Trip Planning
2-Day Varanasi Itinerary
An hour-by-hour plan for backpackers with limited time
Published: 2026-07-29 | Author: Mosaic Hostel Team

Summary

A practical, hour-by-hour 2-day Varanasi itinerary covering the ghats, Sarnath, the old city lanes, and the Ganga Aarti — built for backpackers with limited time.

Two days is tight for Varanasi, but enough to genuinely experience the city's essentials if you plan it well. Here's exactly how to spend it.

Day 1: The Ghats and the Old City

5:30 AM — Sunrise at the ghats. Start at Assi Ghat for the Subah-e-Banaras ceremony, then walk north along the waterfront as the city wakes up. This is the single best free experience Varanasi offers — don't sleep through it.

7:00 AM — Breakfast. Grab kachori-sabzi or poha from a stall near the ghat, or a proper sit-down breakfast at a cafe if you'd rather ease in. Budget ₹50-150.

9:00 AM — Sunrise boat ride (if you didn't already fold this into your walk). Alternatively, use this window to check into your accommodation if you arrived overnight.

11:00 AM — Rest during the heat (especially April-June — see our seasonal guide). Use this block for a shower, a nap, or working through email if you're a remote traveler.

2:00 PM — Walk the old city lanes. Head toward Vishwanath Gali and the market area near Kashi Vishwanath Temple. Expect narrow, crowded lanes, temples tucked between shops, and some of the city's best street food.

4:00 PM — Kashi Vishwanath Temple. Budget an hour including the security queue. Dress modestly.

6:00 PM — Ganga Aarti at Dashashwamedh Ghat. Arrive by 6:00 PM to get a good position before the crowds peak. The ceremony itself runs about 45 minutes.

8:00 PM — Dinner in the old city or back near your accommodation.

Day 2: Sarnath and Reflection

6:00 AM — Optional second sunrise if you want another ghat morning, or sleep in if Day 1 wore you out — this is your trip, not a checklist.

9:00 AM — Depart for Sarnath. About 30-40 minutes each way — see our Sarnath day trip guide for full details on the Dhamek Stupa, the monastery ruins, and the Ashoka Lion Capital museum.

1:00 PM — Lunch back in Varanasi.

3:00 PM — Slow afternoon. This is the block most 2-day itineraries skip, and it's the one we'd actually recommend keeping: sit at a ghat with a chai, read, people-watch. Varanasi rewards unstructured time more than almost any other Indian city.

5:00 PM — Evening walk or second boat ride, watching the light change over the river.

7:00 PM — Final dinner, ideally somewhere with a rooftop view if you haven't had one yet.

What This Itinerary Deliberately Skips

Two days isn't enough for a deep dive into classical music, a cooking class, or a proper exploration of BHU's campus. If any of those interest you, see our 3-5 day itinerary for a version that makes room for them.

Getting Around During Your 2 Days

Varanasi's old city is largely unnavigable by anything wider than a bicycle, so plan your movement around that. Walking covers the ghats and the lanes near Kashi Vishwanath Temple — nothing else works there anyway. For everything outside the old city (the Sarnath trip, transfers to/from Assi Ghat, a late-night return from Dashashwamedh), auto-rickshaws are the default: expect ₹80-150 for short hops within the city and ₹300-400 for the round trip to Sarnath if you're not using a pre-arranged tour. Cycle-rickshaws work for short old-city-adjacent distances and are cheaper but slower. Boat rides are priced per boat, not per person, so team up with other travelers to bring the per-head cost down — a shared sunrise boat typically runs ₹100-200 per person depending on group size and season.

Money-Saving Tips for a 2-Day Trip

Street food is both the cheapest and often the best food in Varanasi — a full day of eating at stalls and small local joints can run ₹300-500 per person, versus double or triple that at restaurants aimed at tourists. Negotiate auto-rickshaw fares before getting in, or use a ride-hailing app if you have local data — it removes the guesswork entirely. If you're doing the Sarnath trip, ask your hostel about pooling a shared auto with other guests instead of booking a private one; this is one of the easiest 50% cost cuts on this itinerary. Frequently Asked Questions

Is 2 days really enough for Varanasi?

It's enough to hit the essentials — sunrise at the ghats, the Ganga Aarti, the old city lanes, and a Sarnath day trip — but it will feel rushed. Most travelers who do a 2-day version end up wishing they'd budgeted 3-4 days once they're actually here; see our slow travel itinerary if you have any flexibility on dates.

Should I book the Ganga Aarti viewing or just walk in?

You don't need a booking. Arrive at Dashashwamedh Ghat by 6:00 PM for a good standing view, or pay a small boat operator for a seat on the water if you'd rather watch the ceremony from the river — this is worth doing at least once and gives noticeably better photos than the crowded ghat steps.

What should I pack for just 2 days?

Modest clothing that covers shoulders and knees (needed for temple visits), a scarf or light shawl for early-morning boat rides, comfortable closed shoes for the uneven old-city lanes, and a basic first-aid kit. Leave the heavy luggage at your hostel if you're doing the Sarnath day trip — you won't need it.

Practical Notes

Base yourself near Assi Ghat rather than directly at Dashashwamedh — you'll sleep better and it's an easy 15-20 minute auto-rickshaw ride to the evening Aarti. See our transfer guide for arrival logistics and our budget breakdown for what this 2-day trip should cost.
