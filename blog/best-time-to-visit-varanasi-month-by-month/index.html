Best Time to Visit Varanasi: Month-by-Month Guide

Trip Planning
Best Time to Visit Varanasi
A month-by-month guide to weather, festivals, and crowds
Published: 2026-07-29 | Author: Mosaic Hostel Team

Summary

When is the best time to visit Varanasi? A month-by-month breakdown of weather, festivals, and crowd levels to help you plan — including Dev Deepawali, Holi, and why winter isn't always the only good option.

Varanasi is a year-round destination — the ghats, the temples, and the daily rhythm of the city don't stop for weather — but when you visit changes the experience considerably. Here's what to actually expect, month by month.

Winter (October–March): The Peak Season

This is when most travelers come, and for good reason. Daytime temperatures sit comfortably between 15–25°C, mornings are cool enough for a jacket at the ghats, and the light — especially at sunrise — is soft and hazy in a way that photographs beautifully. Evenings can get genuinely cold by Varanasi standards (down to 5–8°C in December and January), so pack a warm layer if you're doing the early Subah-e-Banaras ceremony at Assi Ghat.

The trade-off is crowds and price. This is peak season for both domestic and international travelers, so accommodation books up faster and rates are at their highest. If you're planning a visit in November or December, book at least 2–3 weeks ahead — see our budget breakdown for what to expect to pay.

Best for: first-time visitors, photographers, anyone prioritizing comfort over budget or solitude.

Summer (April–June): Hot, Cheap, and Quiet

Summer in Varanasi is genuinely intense — daytime highs regularly hit 40–45°C, and even the mornings are warm. This isn't a season to underestimate: heatstroke is a real risk if you're walking the ghats at midday without water and shade.

That said, summer has real advantages if you can handle the heat. Hostels and hotels drop their rates significantly, the ghats and temples are far less crowded, and the early morning and evening hours — when most of the city's actual life happens anyway — are still very much workable. Plan your day around the heat: out at sunrise, indoors or resting from around 11 AM to 4 PM, out again in the evening.

Best for: budget travelers, remote workers who can structure their day around the heat, anyone who prioritizes empty ghats over comfortable weather.

Monsoon (July–September): Humid, Green, Least Crowded

Monsoon rain in Varanasi arrives in bursts rather than constant drizzle — expect heavy downpours followed by clear, humid stretches. The Ganga rises noticeably during this period and can flood the lower steps of several ghats, including parts of Assi Ghat, which changes how boat rides and ghat walks work (boats often can't run at all during peak flood weeks). Humidity is high and mosquitoes are more of a factor, so pack repellent.

This is the quietest tourist season by a wide margin, and the city looks genuinely different — greener surroundings, a wet-stone shine on the ghats, dramatic skies. It's not for everyone, but travelers who don't mind rain and want to see a version of Varanasi almost no one photographs often come away with a memorable trip.

Best for: experienced India travelers, off-season budget seekers, anyone specifically wanting to avoid crowds.

Festival Calendar Worth Planning Around

Varanasi's festival calendar can make or break a trip, depending on whether you want to be in the middle of it or well clear of it.

Dev Deepawali (Kartik Purnima, typically November) is the single biggest event of the year — the ghats are lit with hundreds of thousands of oil lamps, and it draws enormous crowds from across India. If you want to experience it, book accommodation months in advance; if you'd rather avoid the crush, plan your trip for the weeks just before or after.

Maha Shivratri (February or March) draws large crowds of pilgrims to the city's Shiva temples, including Kashi Vishwanath, with all-night celebrations and processions.

Holi (March) is celebrated with real intensity in Varanasi, particularly in the old city lanes and at BHU. It's a genuinely fun, chaotic experience, but be prepared for a very different, much rowdier city for a few days.

Ganga Mahotsav (around Dev Deepawali, November) is a multi-day cultural festival of classical music and dance performed at the ghats — worth attending if your dates line up, and low-effort to catch since it happens right where you'd already be.

Our Recommendation

If this is your first trip and you want the classic Varanasi experience with manageable weather, aim for late October through February, avoiding the exact Dev Deepawali week unless that's specifically what you're after. If you're on a tighter budget or specifically want to avoid crowds, May–June or August–September are worth serious consideration — just come prepared for the heat or the rain.
